About

Scholar of Water, Cities & Governance

Ayushi Chauhan is a PhD Candidate in the Department of History at Boston University, where she specializes in South Asian and environmental history. Her research sits at the intersection of histories of water, disease, development, space, and governance.

Her dissertation argues that interventions around water are central to a deeply contextualized history of governance, contingency, vision, inequality, and development in the colonial and postcolonial world — newly accounting for the many tensions between environmental governance and the imagination of shared spaces.

She received a Master's in Development Studies from the Indian Institute of Technology, Guwahati, and a B.A. in History and Political Science from Miranda House, Delhi University. She has independently conducted primary research across three continents, drawing on archival research and GIS spatial analysis. She is also co-organizing a symposium on "Asia's Agrarian and Environmental Pasts" at Boston University in December 2026.

Research

Areas of Inquiry

01

Environmental Governance

Exploring tensions between environmental governance and the imagination of shared urban spaces, tracing how state interventions shaped inequality and development across South Asian cities.

02

Disease, Sanitation & Colonial Urbanism

Examining epidemic and endemic disease, colonial administration, and urban transformation — from nineteenth-century epidemics through to sanitary reform in the planning of New Delhi in the twentieth century.

03

Rivers & Urban Commons

Tracing how development pressures reshaped rivers like the Yamuna into contested urban commons, and how communities across Delhi, Lucknow, and Gujarat pushed back.

04

South Asian Modernity

Situating India's urban transformations within broader histories of colonial modernity, infrastructural development, and transnational knowledge about water, sanitation, and public health.

Spatial & Digital History

Railways, City, Environment: A Spatial History of Colonial Delhi, 1857–1931

Historical GIS · Independent Research Project

Combines historical GIS, georeferenced colonial maps, and archival sources to trace urban and environmental transformation in Delhi between 1857 and 1931. Draws on primary sources from the National Archives of India and the British Library India Office Records.
